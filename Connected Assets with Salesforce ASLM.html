The turbine tells you first.

A 4.5 MW turbine runs 260 feet up, in a field, alone. Nobody is watching it — which is why the first sign of trouble is usually a truck roll, a lost day of generation, or a gearbox that didn't have to fail. This walkthrough follows one alarm from the sensor that raised it to the technician who cleared it, across every screen it touched.

The asset knows before you do

Oil temperature and vibration drift for hours before anything fails. That signal already exists — it just doesn't reach anyone who can act on it.

The trip is the expensive part

An hour of diagnosis costs a day of travel, a crane window, and generation that never comes back. Whoever goes needs to know what they'll find before they leave.

The context doesn't travel

Readings live in SCADA, coverage lives in the contract, the last repair lives in someone's memory. The tech arrives with a work order that says "investigate noise."

What each screen was for.

Four audiences, one record underneath. Nobody was asked to open a system they don't already live in.

Why it's safe to let the asset start the work.

Automating the watch is easy. What makes it deployable is where the judgment stays.

Thresholds are configured, not guessed

Every limit sits on the asset model — oil temperature, vibration, derate. The agent compares readings to those numbers. It does not decide on its own what "too hot" means.

The agent drafts, a person approves

The work order, the priority and the steps all arrive written. Nothing is created, nobody is dispatched and no customer is contacted until someone clicks Approve.

The readings travel with the job

The numbers that tripped the alarm are carried onto the work order and into the pre-work brief, so the technician reads the sensor's words rather than a dispatcher's paraphrase.

Every message is on the record

The text to the site manager and the post in the ops channel are both logged against the asset with their channel and timestamp. Six months later the audit trail still answers who was told what.
