Seven handoffs, or one record.

Onboarding lives in a portal or a shared inbox. Bids live in email. Dispatch lives in a phone call. Invoices land in AP. Performance is a spreadsheet somebody rebuilds every quarter. Every handoff is a place where a certificate quietly lapses, an out-of-contract rate gets paid, or a partner nobody has scored keeps getting the work. The seams are where it costs money.

Nobody owns the handoff

Onboarding sits in one place, bids in email, invoices in AP. Because no single record spans them, a lapsed certificate or an out-of-contract rate only surfaces after the work is already done and the crew has left site.

Cheapest is not lowest cost

When credentials and history are not on the same screen as the number, the award goes to the lowest bid. That cost reappears as a re-visit, a failed audit, or an SLA credit — usually in a different budget line.

You cannot manage what you never scored

A partner network you do not employ still carries your SLA and your brand. If performance is a quarterly spreadsheet, you find out who your worst contractor is some time after you renewed them.

Four things that make it hold.

Contractor management is easy to demo and hard to trust. These are the four mechanisms a partner-operations lead will ask about.

Credentials are dated, not filed

An insurance certificate or an arc-flash card carries an effective end date, so it is a scheduling fact rather than a PDF. A partner whose certificate expires before the service date is not offered the work — and the exclusion, with its reason, stays on the record.

Bids are records, not emails

Every invited partner gets a bid record, whether they answer or not. The three you did not pick sit next to the one you did, with the numbers and the reason, so the award has a trail that survives an audit and a change of staff.

Crews close on the surface they already use

The completion contract is identical whether the job closes in the mobile app, by text, from Slack, or through Claude. That matters more for contractors than for employees: you cannot mandate an app rollout to a workforce you do not employ.

The invoice is matched, not trusted

Lines are checked against the work order, the completion record and the signed rate card before a human sees them. Everything that agrees passes silently; the exception is the only thing anyone has to look at.
